WavePool help

Turn a short recording into an instrument.

Quick start

  1. Record a few seconds. Touch Record, lift your finger to start, touch and lift again to stop.
  2. Touch the recording to hear it, and slide to scrub it like tape. Lift your finger to pick that spot as your wave.
  3. Play it on the note pad, or on a MIDI keyboard.
  4. Save it with the Sounds button. Help, under More, explains everything else.

What WavePool does

WavePool turns a short recording into a playable instrument. You record a few seconds of a voice or any sound, slide a finger along it like tape under a playhead, and lift your finger to pick a spot. The wave at that spot becomes the sound you play on the note pad, or on a MIDI keyboard connected to your device.

Recording

On the first screen, touch Record. If VoiceOver is on, it says Record. Lift your finger and recording starts. To stop, touch again, which gives a light tap, and lift, which gives a stronger one. Recording stops by itself after 10 seconds. A short recording is fine. A few seconds of one steady sound works well for pitched instruments, and singing or humming a single note gives a clear pitch. The Input row chooses the microphone. It shows under the Record button when your device has more than one, and it is always under More. The built-in microphone is used unless you change it. Swipe up or down on a row to change it.

Scrubbing

After recording you land on the scrub pad. Touch it anywhere and the recording plays from that spot at normal speed. Slide a finger sideways and it scrubs like tape: left is earlier, right is later, faster is higher in pitch and slower is lower. If you move at about the speed it was recorded, you feel a tap and it locks to normal speed for a moment. A small tick marks the very start and end. Stop moving for a moment and it plays on from there, looping round at the end. A new touch also waits a moment before playing, so you can slide slowly without a burst of sound first. Pad wait, in Sound settings, sets both waits, from 0 (plays at once) to 5 (long). Lift your finger to pick the wave. If it was playing on by itself, the wave is wherever the playhead had got to. If you were scrubbing, it is the spot under your finger. To hear it from the beginning, touch the far left edge.

Playing

The note pad is a row of 10 notes in the key and scale you choose. Touch to play, slide to another note to change note, and use several fingers for chords. Higher on the pad is louder. In Sound settings you can change the key, the scale and the octave. A connected MIDI keyboard also plays the wave, with its pitch bend, mod wheel and sustain pedal.

Scanning the wave

WavePool does not use only one wave from your pick. It makes 33 waves from the area around it, and the sound can slowly move through them. Scan mode in Sound settings chooses how. Wobble moves back and forth by itself. Sweep up and Sweep down move once. Mod wheel lets a MIDI mod wheel do it. Finger scan lets the height of each finger on the note pad do it. Scan width is how far it travels, and Scan speed is how fast. Set width to 0 for a steady sound. The frame strip, down the left edge of the screen, moves through those waves by hand. Hold a note, touch the strip and slide up or down: up goes later in the recording, down goes earlier. You feel a tap at each wave and a stronger one at the middle. When you lift, the waves are rebuilt around where you stopped, so that spot becomes the new middle and the scan can run on both sides of it. A quick tap on the strip, without sliding, goes back to the spot you first picked. The strip does nothing unless a note is being held.

Sound settings and effects

Sound settings is in groups. Envelope: attack is how fast a note fades in, decay lets a held note fall toward the sustain level, and release is how long it takes to fade out. With decay off, a held note stays at full level. With decay on and sustain at 0, a note dies away by itself, like a key or a pluck. Tone: brightness, which dims the top of the sound at a fixed pitch whatever note you play, Soften highs, which tames the high harmonics of every note by the same amount, whatever its pitch, Filter follow, which makes a note start dark and brighten as it comes in and darken as it fades, Unison detune, which adds two slightly out-of-tune copies for a thick sound, one sharp copy toward the left ear and one flat copy toward the right, Unison spread, which sets how far apart those copies sit, from all in the middle at 0 to fully apart at 10, and Frame spread, which makes each ear read a slightly different place along the recording, so the sound widens with no effect added. Frame spread works best on a recording that changes over time, such as a voice. Movement: the scan settings, and vibrato depth and speed. The decay, filter, unison and vibrato settings all start switched off. Effects has chorus, flanger or phaser for movement, then delay and reverb. A new recording starts as the plain wave with no effects. Flicking through saved sounds: touch the Sound button and swipe up or down to step through your saved sounds, newest at the top. Each name is spoken as you go, and the sound loads when you pause. Double tap opens the Sounds screen, where you save, rename, delete and export. Quick flicks: for a quick change with VoiceOver, touch the Settings or Effects button and swipe up or down, without double tapping. Up on Settings gives the lively sound: soft fades, a gentle wobble through the waves and a rounded top end. Down gives the plain wave again. Up on Effects adds a little chorus and reverb, and down turns every effect off. Without VoiceOver, press and hold the button. A double tap opens the full screen. Every row works by swiping up or down with VoiceOver. If you are not using VoiceOver, tap a row to step through its values.

Sounds and saving

WavePool remembers your last recording and settings by itself. The Sounds button saves a sound with a name, and brings it back later. A sound is the picked spot and all your settings, and several sounds can share one recording. With a saved sound loaded, Update changes it, and Save as a new sound makes a variation. Swipe up or down on a saved sound for Rename or Delete. Export wavetable makes a WAV file of the waves, which you can send to Files, AirDrop or other apps, for synths such as Serum or Vital. A noise recording, such as a scrape or hiss, becomes a gritty tone and not a recognisable scrape.

Using VoiceOver

The Record button, the scrub pad and the note pad are direct touch areas. Touch and hold them to use them, with no double tap. Everything else works the usual way. All the buttons are in one row along the bottom of the screen, or in a column down the right edge when the screen is held sideways: Sound, Settings, Effects, New recording and More. With a recording loaded, the first one is called Sound and reads out the name of the current sound, or says unsaved sound. Buttons need a double tap, and rows with a value change by swiping up or down. While a finger is on the note pad or the scrub pad, or while recording, the buttons are switched off and slide partly off the screen, so a stray touch cannot open a screen in the middle of playing. Help, the microphone choices and screen orientation are under More. To close any screen that opened over the pads, make a Z with two fingers, the VoiceOver escape gesture, and VoiceOver goes back to the note pad.

If something is wrong

No sound from the microphone: allow microphone access for WavePool in the iPhone Settings app, then check the Input row under More. Hissing on a recording: switch the input to the iPhone microphone. A Bluetooth headset microphone sounds poor, so Bluetooth microphones is off unless you turn it on. The screen will not turn sideways: use the Screen orientation row under More.
