WavePool help

Turn a short recording into an instrument.

Quick start

  1. Record a few seconds. Touch Record, lift your finger to start, touch and lift again to stop.
  2. Slide a finger along the recording like tape. Lift your finger to pick a spot as your wave.
  3. Play it on the note pad, or on a MIDI keyboard.
  4. Save it with the Sounds button. Help explains everything else.

What WavePool does

WavePool turns a short recording into a playable instrument. You record a few seconds of a voice or any sound, slide a finger along it like tape under a playhead, and lift your finger to pick a spot. The wave at that spot becomes the sound you play on the note pad, or on a MIDI keyboard connected to your device.

Recording

On the first screen, touch Record. If VoiceOver is on, it says Record. Lift your finger and recording starts. To stop, touch again, which gives a light tap, and lift, which gives a stronger one. Recording stops by itself after 10 seconds. A short recording is fine. A few seconds of one steady sound works well for pitched instruments, and singing or humming a single note gives a clear pitch. The Input row below the button chooses the microphone. The built-in microphone is used unless you change it. Swipe up or down on a row to change it.

Scrubbing

After recording you land on the scrub pad. Slide a finger sideways to play the recording, the way you drag tape across a playhead. Left is earlier and right is later. Move faster for a higher pitch, and slower for a lower one. If you move at about the speed it was recorded, you feel a tap and it locks to normal speed for a moment. A small tick marks the very start and end. When you lift your finger, that spot is picked as the wave. Hold to play, below the scrub pad, plays the recording over and over while you hold.

Playing

The note pad is a row of 10 notes in the key and scale you choose. Touch to play, slide to another note to change note, and use several fingers for chords. Higher on the pad is louder. In Sound settings you can change the key, the scale and the octave. A connected MIDI keyboard also plays the wave, with its pitch bend, mod wheel and sustain pedal.

Scanning the wave

WavePool does not use only one wave from your pick. It makes 33 waves from the area around it, and the sound can slowly move through them. Scan mode in Sound settings chooses how. Wobble moves back and forth by itself. Sweep up and Sweep down move once. Mod wheel lets a MIDI mod wheel do it. Finger scan lets the height of each finger on the note pad do it. Scan width is how far it travels, and Scan speed is how fast. Set width to 0 for a steady sound.

Sound settings and effects

Sound settings has volume, attack (how fast a note fades in), release (how long it takes to fade out), and brightness. Effects has chorus, flanger or phaser for movement, then delay and reverb. Every row works by swiping up or down with VoiceOver. If you are not using VoiceOver, tap a row to step through its values.

Sounds and saving

WavePool remembers your last recording and settings by itself. The Sounds button saves a sound with a name, and brings it back later. A sound is the picked spot and all your settings, and several sounds can share one recording. With a saved sound loaded, Update changes it, and Save as a new sound makes a variation. Swipe up or down on a saved sound for Rename or Delete. Export wavetable makes a WAV file of the waves, which you can send to Files, AirDrop or other apps, for synths such as Serum or Vital. A noise recording, such as a scrape or hiss, becomes a gritty tone and not a recognisable scrape.

Using VoiceOver

The Record button, the scrub pad, the note pad and Hold to play are direct touch areas. Touch and hold them to use them, with no double tap. Everything else works the usual way. Buttons need a double tap, and rows with a value change by swiping up or down. While a finger is on the note pad, the buttons around it are switched off, so a stray touch cannot open a screen in the middle of playing.

If something is wrong

No sound from the microphone: allow microphone access for WavePool in the iPhone Settings app, then check the Input row. Hissing on a recording: switch the input to the iPhone microphone. A Bluetooth headset microphone sounds poor, so Bluetooth microphones is off unless you turn it on. The screen will not turn sideways: use the Screen orientation row.
